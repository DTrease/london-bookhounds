The papers of the Bookhounds of P. L. Westoby’s, New Bond Street, as they recorded them. The shop deals in manuscripts and printed literature, and its trade has taken its staff into stranger company than a bookseller has any right to expect.

In Chapter One, Gallery of the One-Eyed God, a telegram from Mr. Westoby called the Bookhounds to the Pimander Club in Piccadilly, where Johnstone Dives engaged them to find a copy of the Caedis Urbi Verae Claves. The search led them from a flat in Kensington to a public house in Spitalfields, and it ended with the ancient book up in flames. Chapter Two, The Shadow on the Throne, is in preparation. It opens in 1936, a year when the whole country is talking about the King.

A word on the use of this book. Sign the borrower’s register with the name of your Bookhound, and the book will know whose copy it is. Each player may alter only the sheet of their own Bookhound, by pressing “Edit this sheet” at the foot of it. Everyone else may read it. “Return the book” at the top of the page signs you out of your own copy and leaves everyone else’s as it was.

Any page may be folded by pressing its running head. The Contents lists every page and will take you to it. Plates may be dragged and enlarged, with places visited pinned upon it; hold a pointer over a pin, or touch it, to read of the place. Pressing an ability on a sheet opens a telegram form for a roll, and every throw is entered in Appendix D, where the whole table can see it.

The shop stands on a busy corner of New Bond Street. It is well kept. Books fill every wall up to the high ceilings, wheeled ladders run along the left and right walls, and a gallery at the back looks down over the front of the shop. A coat room opens off the entrance. From it a crawlspace runs through to the shop next door, and it feels longer than it should.

The shop has electric light, which the owner arranged by paying the right officials. The parlour does not. It is still lit by paraffin lamps, because the owner didn’t want electricians lifting the rugs. Under them are five spots of candle wax on the floorboards, joined by thin carved lines in the shape of a pentagram. The parlour is used for book clubs and readings; the rest of the time, only close friends of the shop are let in.

Stairs in the parlour go down to the cellar store room, which has a needlessly complicated wall safe, made by a firm nobody can remember without reading the name off the door. On the roof, the owner keeps a shed where he looks after a small flock of pigeons and a small observatory.

A telegram from Westoby brings the Bookhounds to the Pimander Club. There Johnstone Dives hires them to find the de Castries copy of the Caedis Urbi Verae Claves, a book said to curse any house that keeps it. Ronaldo follows a one-eyed vagrant to a stone door in John’s Walk and chalks a frog’s-eye sign on it. Meanwhile the astronomer Paul Fletcher is on his way from Cape Town to meet the art dealer Aston Drummel.

Bob and Ronaldo break into Drummel’s flat in Kensington. They take a Ten Bells coaster, a note from Fletcher with a symbol Bob remembers from the Boer War, and some canvases by an unknown painter called Francis Bacon. Magwich warns them to stay away from Drummel. White eyes start appearing on walls in Whitechapel. At the Ten Bells, Bacon tells everyone to bid at his auction.

In Bacon’s lodgings something climbs out of a painting and asks, “Who sent you?” It only backs off when they give Dives’s name. Drummel and Magwich turn up at the shop and are seen off with a shotgun. Bastion Melwig brings On the Glory of Nodens, which tells of an old British god, Nodens, at war with Byatis, a one-eyed frog-god under London.

Bob follows the address on an old silver key to a house with his own family’s portrait on the wall, a map of Whitechapel pinned in the shape of an eye, and letters from Dives. He realises he is Westoby. Charlie finds a letter by D’Onstan claiming the Ripper murders were a ritual. Left alone with the canvas, Bob agrees to help destroy the followers of Nodens and disappears into it.

Ronaldo gives one of his eyes to the emissary of Byatis. In return he can pass through Byatis’s doors.

The Bookhounds burn the canvas, and Ronaldo disappears. Crowley tells them the Caedis Urbi Verae Claves can open or close the Eye of Byatis. At the auction in the Ten Bells, Ronaldo bursts in with the vagrants, Guzzy shoots Fletcher and then Ronaldo, and Frankie throws the book on the fire.
